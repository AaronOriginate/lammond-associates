Get in Touch View Results

Here's what normally happens. You brief an agency. They say all the right things. Then silence. Three weeks later, a batch of CVs arrives — half of them irrelevant, a couple that are close, and one that makes you wonder if they read the brief at all. You chase for feedback. Nothing. You chase again. A hurried email. The process drags on for months and you end up either compromising or starting over.

That's not bad luck. It's a business model. Most agencies work contingent — they only get paid on placement, so they're running fifteen searches at once and hoping one sticks. The maths doesn't work in your favour. I do it differently.

The Search Process

Six stages. Full visibility.

Stage 1: The Brief — Getting It Right (Week 1)

Most recruiters take a brief over a 30-minute call and start sourcing the same afternoon. I don't. Before I look at a single candidate, I need to understand three things: what the role actually needs, what your team is like, and what's happened before. I'll ask questions that might surprise you — about team dynamics, about what the last person got wrong, about what success looks like in six months rather than on day one.

Stage 2: Market Mapping — The Longlist (Weeks 1-2)

I map the market. Not a keyword search on LinkedIn. Proper identification of who's out there, where they are, and whether they'd move. The difference between a specialist and a generalist shows up here. A generalist searches for 'supply chain director' and gets 10,000 results. I know the 30 people in this market who could do your specific role — and I probably already know which five would be interested. You'll see the longlist. No black box.

Stage 3: Candidate Engagement (Weeks 2-3)

I reach out to candidates personally. Not through a script. Not through a mass InMail campaign. Senior supply chain professionals don't respond to generic messages from recruiters they've never heard of. They respond to someone who can talk about MRP implementations, or S&OP cadences, or why the interim market has shifted since IR35 reform.

Stage 4: The Shortlist — Quality Over Volume (Weeks 3-4)

You'll receive a shortlist. Typically three to five candidates. Each one with a written assessment — not a recycled CV summary, but my honest view of their strengths, their gaps, and why I think they're right for your specific role. If I can't give you a shortlist where every candidate is genuinely credible, I'll tell you why.

Stage 5: Interview Support (Weeks 4-6)

This is where most agencies vanish. They've sent the CVs, their job is done, and they sit back hoping for a placement fee. I stay involved. I'll prep candidates, gather feedback from both sides, and if something isn't working, I'll tell you directly. If a candidate has concerns they haven't voiced — I'll know about it.

Stage 6: Offer & Onboarding (Weeks 6-8)

The offer stage is where deals fall apart. Counter-offers. Cold feet. Unrealistic notice periods. I've seen all of it across 20 years. And I don't stop at the signature. The first 90 days determine whether a placement sticks. I'll check in with both you and the candidate to make sure the reality matches the promise.

Your Part

What I Need From You

Good recruitment is a two-way process. It doesn't work if I'm brilliant and you're unavailable.

Time for the brief. An hour at the start. Not a rushed call between meetings. The brief shapes everything — getting it wrong costs weeks.

Honest feedback, fast. When I send you a shortlist, I need feedback within a few days — not three weeks. Candidates lose interest. Good ones get snapped up.

Access to decision-makers. If someone other than you makes the final call, I need to understand their criteria too. Surprises at the offer stage are expensive.

Commitment to getting it right. You have choices to make, and I offer service levels to match your commitment. A half-hearted search gets half-hearted results.

Service Levels

Service matched to your commitment

Permanent Search

Contingent model for mid-to-senior permanent hires. You get a specialist with 20 years of supply chain context, not a generalist running your search alongside fourteen others. Ideal when you have a clearly defined role and reasonable timelines.

Retained & Executive Search

Staged fee structure for director-level and business-critical hires. Dedicated market mapping, competitor analysis, salary benchmarking, and a replacement guarantee. The right model when the hire genuinely matters.

Interim & Contract

Experienced interim leaders who can start within days. Supply chain directors, transformation consultants, procurement specialists. IR35-compliant, properly assessed, and genuinely senior. For when you can't wait 12 weeks.

A Note on Timelines

I won't promise you a placement in two weeks. Anyone who does is either lying or not being thorough.

For a senior permanent role, expect 6-8 weeks from brief to offer — sometimes faster if the market is favourable, sometimes longer if the role is niche. For interim, it can be days. For executive search, 8-12 weeks is realistic for a thorough process.

These aren't arbitrary numbers. They reflect twenty years of learning what "fast enough" actually means — quick enough that you don't lose candidates to other opportunities, thorough enough that you don't end up back here in six months making the same hire again.
